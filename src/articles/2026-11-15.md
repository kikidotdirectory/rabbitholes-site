pronounced 'Karch-miska'

It seems to have an article because it is the "seat" of a [gmina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmina_Karczmiska), which are the municipalities of Poland. As of 2025, Poland has 2,479 of them. There are 3 types of gmina:

- urban municipalities, "constituted either by a standalone town or one of the 107 cities, the latter governed by a city mayor,"[^1]
- urban-rural municipalities, which consist of a town and surrounding villages and countryside. these municipalities almost always have their seat in the town (with the exception of [Gmina Nowe Skalmierzyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmina_Nowe_Skalmierzyce), which had its seat moved to a neighboring village for an unspecified reason),
- and rural municipalities, which exist as villages and surrounding countryside, of which Karczmiska is.

The gmina operate as a [mayor-council government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor%E2%80%93council_government), which is apparently one of the most common forms of local government in the United States (which is news to me). In these kinds of systems (in the 'strong-mayor' variant), the mayor is given almost complete control over the people within their administration, being able to appoint/dismiss heads, though some limitations may exist based on location. This feels kind of similar to the relationship between the Executive and Legislative branches of the federal government.

What I assume that '66 cities with county rights' means is that these cities do not control other land, the entire municipality _is_ the city. The remainder exist as the heads of the counties that they are in and the remainder of the urban municipalities have similar structure, just that the seats of them are towns.

The responsibilities of each municipality are distinguished by two 'classes': 'own tasks,' which are public tasks funded by themselves to 'satisfy the needs of the community' and 'commissioned tasks' which come from the central government and come with 'appropriate mandatory government subvention to support them.'

These tasks can each be distinguished again by classifying them as 'compulsory,' tasks that cannot be abstained from & must be budgeted for (i.e. basic public benefits) and 'optional,' which can be carried out based of the available budget & according to local needs.
